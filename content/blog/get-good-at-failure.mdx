You fail. I fail. We all fail.

Yet, from childhood, we are taught to fear failure. "Don't fail. You have to succeed."

But what if we looked at failure differently?

You cannot become a success story without the experiences that shape you along the way.

I have had my own share of challenging chapters in life. At the time, I didn't know where they would lead me. I simply had to keep going, keep learning and keep finding my way.

Looking back, I realise that some of the experiences I once struggled with became lessons that shaped who I am today.

That changed the way I see failure.

When something doesn't work, I try to ask:

- What can I learn from this?
- What can I do differently?
- What's my next step?

And then I move forward.

I call it *amnesia* to failure.

*Not because I forget the lesson—but because I don't want the failure to become my identity.*

Perhaps what we fear most isn't failure itself. Maybe it's embarrassment: what others might think when we try and don't succeed.

But what if we cared less about looking perfect and became more comfortable with learning?

Take the big swing. Sometimes you will miss.

Learn. Adjust. Try again.

That's not failure. That's growth in motion.

And be mindful of what you give your attention to. Surround yourself with stories, people and reminders that make it easier to believe that learning, starting over, and trying again are all part of the journey.

Because failure doesn't have to be the opposite of success.

Sometimes, failure is simply one of the roads that takes us there.

Fail. Learn. Adjust. Rise.

And begin again.

## Reflection into Action

There is a beautiful thought attributed to Ralph Waldo Emerson:

> Plant a tree under whose shade you do not plan to sit.

I love that idea because it captures something so profound about purpose.

Perhaps our purpose is not always about what we will receive, achieve, or experience for ourselves. Sometimes, it is about what we are willing to create, nurture, and leave behind for someone else.

To plant a tree knowing that another person may one day sit beneath its shade—that is an act of faith. It is saying that I may not be here to experience the fruit of this, but I trust that what I do today can make someone else's tomorrow a little better.

And perhaps that is what a meaningful life is about.

We listen. We learn. We grow. We discover our gifts—and then we use those gifts in service of something larger than ourselves.

Because maybe the question is not only, "Why am I here?"

Maybe the deeper question is:

"What am I here to leave behind?"

- What shade will exist because I was here?
- What life might be changed because I showed up?
- Whose story might be different because I chose to listen?
- What will continue growing long after I am gone?

So I invite you to stop for a moment and ask yourself—not what the world wants from you, not what you think you are supposed to accomplish, but:

What is calling me?

And then ask the harder question:

Am I willing to follow it?

Because your life is already planting something.

The question is whether you are planting it consciously.

*Plant the tree.*
*Tell the story.*
*Answer the call.*
*And leave behind a little more shade than you found.*

## Bringing them together

"Get Good at Failure" reminds us that failure is an experience, not an identity. We can learn from what didn't work without allowing it to define who we are.

"Reflection into Action" takes that learning a step further. It invites us to look beyond our own achievements and ask how we can use our experiences, gifts, and wisdom to make a difference in someone else's life.

Together, they create a powerful progression: learn from life, grow through experience, and use that growth to serve a purpose greater than yourself.
